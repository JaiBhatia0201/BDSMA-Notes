## 2. Use BOTH my PPTs and the internet

This is a critical requirement.

**Do not restrict yourself to the professor's PPTs.** Use them as the primary guide to the syllabus, topic coverage, terminology, and course expectations. However, do not blindly trust every slide as complete, current, or correct.

Use web research whenever necessary to:
- Verify inaccurate, ambiguous, incomplete, or outdated slide content.
- Expand topics that are only briefly mentioned.
- Explain difficult concepts using better, more accessible explanations.
- Find reliable examples, diagrams, and practical applications.
- Check current features and terminology of platforms and tools.
- Resolve conflicting definitions or technical claims.

### Research source preferences

Prefer:
1. Official documentation, standards bodies, government/institutional publications, and original specifications.
2. Peer-reviewed research papers, recognized textbooks, and university resources.
3. Reputable technical publishers and established industry sources.

Avoid relying on random blogs, SEO-generated articles, copied notes, or unsourced claims when better sources are available.

For changing platform features (such as Google Analytics, YouTube Studio, Meta Business Suite, and social listening tools), prioritize current official documentation. For foundational concepts, prioritize authoritative textbooks, original research, and university sources.

### Research integrity

- Cite external information with working links and source names/titles.
- Do not fabricate sources, URLs, authors, dates, statistics, or quotations.
- Do not present an unverified claim as fact.
- Distinguish facts from examples, estimates, interpretations, and debated claims.
- If reputable sources disagree, explain the disagreement.
- If browsing is unavailable, clearly state that the claim could not be independently verified.
- Explain sources in your own words; do not copy large passages.

### If a PPT seems wrong or unclear

Do not silently copy it or silently replace its wording. Use this format:

**PPT says:** [the wording or claim from the slide]

**Verified clarification:** [what reliable sources support, with explanation]

**What I should say in the viva:** [a safe, accurate, examiner-ready answer]

If the slide's wording may be what the professor expects, preserve it as the course wording while clearly explaining any technical correction or qualification.

### Algorithms and processes

- Step-by-step procedure.

### Architecture and diagrams
Use Mermaid diagrams where supported and helpful. Explain every component, arrow, stage, and data flow in simple language. Do not include invented architecture details.

### Tools and platforms
Explain what the tool is, what it does, why it is used in this subject, its important features, and a realistic example. Verify current platform-specific features through official documentation where relevant. Distinguish current features from historical or course-specific versions.
